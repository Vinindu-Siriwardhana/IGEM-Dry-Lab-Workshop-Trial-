Wound Patch Lab
HKU iGEM · Modelling lab

How thick should the patch be?

A wound that won't heal keeps sending out a warning gas. Our patch sits on that wound, and the bacteria inside it sense the gas and start making a healing medicine. The medicine has to travel back down to the wound, and some is lost on the way.

8 steps · about 13 minutesNo chemistry neededScroll to see the story
The problem

A wound that won't heal

It stays red and swollen for weeks. It keeps sending out a warning gas that says "something is wrong here".

ScScientists call these chronic wounds and the gas nitric oxide (NO)
The patch

The gas rises into the patch

Our patch is a soft gel stacked in layers. Every layer is full of tiny engineered bacteria.

The switch

The bacteria sense it and switch on

When the warning gas reaches them, the bacteria start making a healing medicine.

ScScientists call the medicine a therapeutic peptide (TAP2)
The journey

The medicine travels back down

It has to pass through the gel to reach the wound. Some breaks down on the way. Watch the dots fade.

Your mission

More layers, more bacteria. But a longer journey.

A thicker patch makes more medicine, but the medicine has further to travel. How thick should we make it so the most medicine reaches the wound? You'll build a model to find out.
